The story

Forty years under one gold head.

For a generation of Houstonians, Magic Island was where you went to dress up: a private club of Vegas-style magic shows, close-up magicians at the table, prime rib, and a pharaoh watching the traffic go by on US-59.

The building has stayed a landmark through every year it was dark. Drivers on the upper deck of the Southwest Freeway still point at the head. The new owners kept the Egyptian rooms, the columns and the murals, and rebuilt what the storm and the fire took.

  1. 1984Magic Island opens on the Southwest Freeway near Greenbriar: magic theatre, dining rooms, psychics and palm readers, and a dress code.
  2. 2008Hurricane Ike, then a fire. The club closes and the building sits empty for sixteen years.
  3. 2018Houston physician Dr. Mohammad Athari buys the property with a plan to bring it back.
  4. NowRenovation complete behind the original facade. Staff are being hired. The next act is being scheduled.

What's coming

The rooms of the new Magic Island.

One address, four ways to spend an evening.

Dinner

Dining rooms with the velvet, the gilt frames and the green walls kept from the original club. A table here comes with a magician who stops by between courses.

The Theatre

An intimate theatre upstairs for magic, comedians and touring acts, the way the original drew performers from around the world.

Drinks

A new bar and a covered patio for the hours before and after the show.

The Ballroom

A full ballroom for weddings, galas, company dinners and expos, with the club's own kitchen, bar and entertainment on the same floor.
